An electroencephalogram, or EEG, records the small electrical signals your brain cells use to communicate. Sensors placed on the scalp pick up these signals and show them as wavy lines, one line for each area of the brain.

## What the lines show

Healthy brains produce recognisable rhythms that change when you are awake, drowsy or asleep. A neurologist looks at how these rhythms are shaped, how fast they are and whether both sides of the brain behave in a similar way.

Some conditions leave a signature in the recording, such as brief sharp waves between seizures. Others show up as a general slowing of activity.

## Common reasons for a referral

- Investigating possible seizures or epilepsy
- Understanding episodes of confusion, fainting or unusual behaviour
- Monitoring known epilepsy and the effect of medication
- Assessing brain function after a head injury or infection

An EEG is one piece of the picture. Your doctor will combine it with your history, an examination and sometimes imaging such as MRI.
